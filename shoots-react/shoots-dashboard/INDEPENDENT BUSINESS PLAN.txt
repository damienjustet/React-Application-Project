II. PROBLEM
Financial[b][c] Applications are Overwhelming
Users turn to financial apps for guidance and organization amid the clutter of their finances, from advanced day traders to teenagers. Catering to such a broad scope is a challenge for financial apps that promise simplicity. Many financial apps technically offer features users need, but the sheer volume of options becomes overwhelming, creating a problem of too many features. According to UXCam’s 2025 Retention Benchmark Report, finance apps retain only 4.6% of users by Day 30, meaning more than 95% of new users churn within the first month. Many note overly complicated onboarding processes that take forever to complete, indicating real issues in the market. The problem isn’t the presence of features; it’s the failure to deliver them in a way that feels intuitive and approachable.


Financial Applications Lack a Personalized Experience
Getting users to download a finance app is easy; keeping them engaged is the real challenge. When an app feels confusing, clunky, or generic, users abandon it. Winning apps do not succeed just because of their services, but because of the rewarding experience they deliver. Yet many financial apps fail to engage. Another 2025 study reports that 96% of users abandon apps within a month due to low engagement and poor personalization. Generic notifications fail to trigger behavioral change, making the app an observer rather than an active financial coach. Nobody opens a finance app for fun, so when the experience isn’t personal or encouraging, it drains the joy from financial health and leaves users with no real sense of progress. The result is wasted energy, lost trust, and yet again, declining retention rates that cripple long-term success. 


Financial Applications Fail to Provide Essential Features
Financial apps often focus so heavily on a niche that they forget the essentials. Others try to solve the problem of overwhelming users with features by cutting what users still rely on, and some simply prioritize monetization over usability. What seemed to be the Swiss Army Knife of their economic life becomes a fragmented experience, causing many to juggle multiple apps just for the basics. A 2025 survey of 1,500+ users found that nearly 60% of working-age adults reported using multiple financial apps because their primary app lacked core features. The absence of essential functionality not only undermines trust but also accelerates abandonment, leaving users frustrated and searching for alternatives that meet their needs. In the end, an app that can’t deliver the basics offers little long-term value.
III. CUST[d]OMER SEGMENTS


Shoots’ primary market comprises Gen Z and Millennials, consumers aged 18 - 41 who account for over 70% of personal finance app users. Our secondary market is Gen X, a group with the highest average debt, who are actively seeking financial clarity.
	

Market Potential
The personal finance apps domain of the fintech industry is one of the fastest-expanding consumer markets in the U.S., with a 7.2% compound annual growth rate and expected to reach roughly $16 billion by 2033. With the widespread adoption of digital finance, millions of Americans are turning to personal finance apps, particularly among Gen Z and Millennials, who are significantly driving this trend. Time spent on personal finance increased by 63% during the pandemic, highlighting a lasting shift towards mobile-first money management. 
Shoots will launch exclusively in the United States. The Total Addressable Market (TAM) comprises ~331 million American smartphone owners. Narrowing to consumers already engaged in digital finance, 146.4 million Americans are active users of Personal Finance Apps, forming Shoots’ Serviceable Available Market (SAM). There has never been a better time for Shoots to capitalize on a booming market that is only growing.


Demographic Segmentation
Age Groups: Gen Z & Millennials
	

Shoots aims to target consumers who seek an enjoyable economic experience — those willing to pay for premium subscription features as a long-term investment. People aged 18 to 41 remain the largest group of finance app users. 
A survey reported that 89% of Gen Z prefer managing finances via mobile apps over visiting a traditional bank. While entering the workforce, one often juggles student loans and first credit cards. Nearly one in four student borrowers is Gen Z, underscoring their need for debt management tools.
85% of Millennials prefer managing their finances online or through an app, making them the most preferred demographic, with Gen Z at 79%. Managing mortgages, family expenses, and retirement plans, Millennials value simplicity. By blending the customized experiences Gen Z craves with the clarity Millennials demand, Shoots positions itself as a worthwhile investment across generations.
        While Millennials and Gen Z are our primary target, Shoots aims towards Gen X as a secondary target market. Often called the “Sandwich Generation”, they are simultaneously those supporting aging parents, children, leaving them with severe Time Poverty. This group has the highest average debt ($157,556), but their deeper challenge is a lack of time to make complex financial decisions. Shoots provides the time-saving structure Gen X needs for a financially healthy life.


Annual Income Disposable Income: $48,000+
	

A financial app subscription averages $77 annually and $11.66 monthly across the market, making it an accessible investment relative to annual income for Gen Z and Millennials. Shoots offers free features and services to all users. Yet, it is more appealing to those who can comfortably afford a premium subscription to financial services, with Gen Z’s median income at $48,532 and Millennials' at $71,161-$89,968. 
With a simple target cost of $6 a month, Shoots balances accessibility for middle‑income earners with premium appeal for higher‑income users, falling at a more affordable rate than competing applications.


Geography: Urban and Suburban
	

Shoots targets individuals in urban and suburban environments, where smartphone penetration and reliance on mobile banking are highest. These areas are the most promising markets for financial app adoption.
Shoots is a digital application available to all smartphone users. However, currently, 75% of users of personal finance applications are urban, with 25% suburban. By focusing on urban and suburban markets, Shoots positions itself at the intersection of accessibility and affordability -- Serving professionals in dense cities while supporting families in suburban communities.
At the same time, many parts of the U.S. face banking deserts (areas with limited access to physical branches). Making mobile-first financial tools is not just convenient for our market, but essential.

V. SOLUTION[s]


It is Shoots' primary objective to put an end to overwhelming, impersonal budgeting and to present a superior alternative through modern management methods. With this product[t], personal finance becomes quick, orderly, and easy, removing the stress and confusion that typically accompany money management. Not only will the user’s financial life become consistently stress‑free and organized, but their money will be maximized to its true potential for future personal plans.[u]
	

Simplify Financial Management
The most significant problem that Shoots solves is the overwhelming nature of personal finance apps. To mitigate this issue, Shoots uses a simple onboarding questionnaire that enables a quick, effortless setup. By asking about your financial goals, current situation, and level of expertise, Shoots immediately creates a customized dashboard and settings tailored to your personal needs, with the option to customize further later.
Imagine a financial app greeting you like a financial advisor, deciding what is in your best interest. The clear, understandable widgets and drag‑and‑drop interface are remarkable, eliminating clutter while maintaining an abundance of features for everyone. Shoots also gives people who struggle to track expenses full automation and bank linking[v], making financial records frictionless.[w]
Personalized Financial[x] Wellness
Onboarding doesn’t just tackle the overwhelming aspects of personal finance; it also addresses the impersonal elements of notifications, tips, and reminders. Through the questionnaire, Shoots determines whether you are a user who loves constant updates, needs a little motivation, or prefers personal streaks to feel at home with the app. Tips and insights are entirely based on your current financial literacy.
Once Shoots is introduced to a user, it becomes their financial companion, helping them feel comfortable with their own growth. With custom themes, colors, and images for your dashboard, you can be reminded of who or what you are saving up for. From saving for a car to visiting a loved family member across the country, custom images never let you forget your goal. On top of that, Shoots’ personalized notifications offer motivating tips and callback reminders to keep your financial health thriving.

Lifetime Values[az] (LTVs)


Monthly Subscription x Retention = LTV
        Lifetime value (LTV) is a benchmark used to estimate the average revenue a customer generates over their lifetime as a buyer. The quantifiable value of a customer serves as the foundation for Shoots in evaluating business decisions, such as marketing, budget, and resource allocation. Additionally, LTVs will contribute to revenue, profit, and liquidity forecasting. Shoots will group its customers into two key categories: “Core Subscribers” and “Referral Subscribers.” Through extensive research, 
Shoots has determined the average business lifespan per customer is 24 months. Core subscribers are the average customer, subscribing for the whole expected retention period at $6 per month, with a gross LTV of $144. Referral subscribers are core subscribers who send links through Shoots’ platform, accounting for a new customer, both paying a gross LTV of $288.
